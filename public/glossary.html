What do these mean?

Every label and code the product uses, in plain English. You can also tap or hover any chip anywhere in the app for a one-line version of the same explanation.

Contacts and the pipeline

Priority 1was: P1

Best-fit contact. The right person at a company that matches the pitch well. Reach out first.

Priority 2was: P2

Good fit, second wave. Contacted after the Priority 1 list is working.

Priority 3was: P3

Long shot or backup contact at a company already covered by someone better placed.

Not yet contactedwas: new

Nothing has been sent to this person yet.

No name yetwas: placeholder

We know the role exists (for example a Head of Sponsorships) but have not found the actual person yet.

Left the companywas: departed

This person moved on from that company, so they will not be contacted.

Duplicate

The same person appears twice in the list; the extra copy is hidden and never contacted.

Email verifiedwas: verified

The address is tied to the person, not just the company, and confirmed deliverable. Safe to send to.

Unverified emailwas: unverified

An address was found but not yet confirmed to work. It is not used for sending until it passes a check.

Ready to emailwas: sendable pool

Contacts with a verified address who are cleared for outreach. The number the whole pipeline feeds.

Emails foundwas: coverage

The share of contacts where an email address has been found at all, verified or not.

Email source

Where an address came from, and what ties it to the person: their own profile, the company domain plus their full name, or only the company. Company-only matches are quarantined.

Industry groupwas: vertical

The market a company belongs to: Airlines, Crypto, Consumer Tech and so on.

Drafts and review

Draft

An email the system wrote and is holding for your approval. Nothing sends itself, ever.

Checks passedwas: linted

The automatic writing checks (length, tone, facts, links) found no problems with the draft.

Needs a lookwas: flagged

The automatic checks raised at least one warning. Read the draft before approving it.

Too similarwas: similarity

The draft reads very close to an email already sent. Repeated wording is a spam signal, so the copy should vary.

1st email, 2nd emailwas: touch 1, touch 2

Which email in the sequence this is for that person: the opener, then the follow-up.

Template A, B, C…was: variant

Which of the rotating wording templates the draft was written from, so no two prospects get identical copy.

In Draftswas: placed

You approved it, and the email was saved into the sending account's Drafts folder. It still has not been sent.

Rejected

You turned the draft down. The reason is recorded so the same mistake is not repeated.

Sending accounts and warmup

Warmup

For the first weeks, the accounts only email each other with harmless notes. Inbox providers watch this traffic and learn to trust the accounts before any real outreach starts.

Outreach accountwas: cold

An account that sends the real emails to prospects once its warmup is done.

Warmup helperwas: support

An account that only sends and receives warmup mail with our own accounts. It never emails prospects.

Volume ramp-upwas: ramp

After warmup, real sending starts tiny and grows slowly, so the volume never looks suspicious.

Parked

An account that is not running yet, either waiting on DNS records or not signed in.

Not signed inwas: no credentials

The site has no saved login for the mailbox, so it cannot place drafts or send warmup mail from it. Connect the mailbox, then it joins the rotation by itself.

Microsoft 365was: Graph

How the site connects to Outlook and Microsoft-hosted mailboxes (through Microsoft's Graph system).

Gmailwas: IMAP

How the site connects to Gmail mailboxes (through the standard IMAP mail protocol).

Automatic runwas: cron

The scheduled job that sends warmup mail by itself several times a day, at irregular times.

Domain records and delivery

Waiting on DNSwas: held, DNS-held

The account is paused because its domain's email records are not live yet. It starts by itself once they are.

DKIM

A DNS record that puts a signature on outgoing mail, proving it really comes from your domain.

DMARC

A DNS record that tells inboxes what to do with mail that fails the DKIM check. Together they keep mail out of spam.

SPF

A DNS record listing which servers are allowed to send mail for a domain. A wrong entry can block everything.

Landed in inboxwas: delivered

The share of our mail that arrived in the inbox rather than the spam folder.
